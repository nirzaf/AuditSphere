# 1. Planning & Risk Assessment

- Obtain company registration documents and basic company information.
- Obtain prior-year audited financial statements and audit report.
- Obtain current-year Trial Balance and draft financial statements.
- Agree opening balances with prior-year audited balances.
- Understand the nature of business, major revenue streams and accounting system.
- Identify significant account balances and transaction classes.
- Identify significant audit risks, fraud risks and areas involving management judgement.
- Determine materiality and performance materiality.
- Prepare the audit strategy and audit program.

# 2. Cash & Bank

- Obtain bank reconciliation for all bank accounts at year-end.
- Agree bank ledger balances with the Trial Balance.
- Agree bank reconciliation balances with year-end bank statements.
- Obtain direct bank confirmations and reconcile confirmed balances.
- Check outstanding cheques, deposits and other reconciling items.
- Investigate old or unusual outstanding items.
- Test selected bank transactions to supporting documents.
- Review subsequent bank statements for unusual transactions.

# 3. Trade Receivables

- Obtain the year-end receivable ageing report.
- Agree total receivables and ageing to the GL/TB.
- Review significant and overdue customer balances.
- Send customer balance confirmations and investigate differences.
- Perform alternative procedures for non-confirmed balances.
- Test selected sales invoices to delivery documents and ledger.
- Check subsequent customer collections through bank statements.
- Perform/recalculate ECL and assess adequacy of provision.
- Test sales and receivable cut-off around year-end.

# 4. Inventory

- Obtain the year-end inventory listing and agree it to the GL.
- Attend/observe physical inventory count where applicable.
- Perform auditor test counts and reconcile differences.
- Check inventory quantities against count sheets/final listing.
- Test inventory costs to purchase invoices or supporting records.
- Review slow-moving, damaged and obsolete inventory.
- Compare cost with NRV where applicable.
- Test purchases and goods received around year-end for cut-off.

# 5. Revenue / Sales

- Obtain sales listing and reconcile total revenue to GL/TB.
- Perform analytical review of monthly and annual sales.
- Select sales samples based on value and risk.
- Check invoices to customer orders/delivery documents.
- Verify quantity, price, calculation and accounting entry.
- Check selected subsequent receipts where relevant.
- Review significant credit notes after year-end.
- Perform revenue cut-off testing before and after year-end.

# 6. Purchases & Trade Payables

- Obtain supplier ageing and reconcile it to the GL/TB.
- Review significant, old and unusual supplier balances.
- Obtain supplier confirmations and investigate differences.
- Compare supplier statements with the company's payable ledger.
- Test selected purchases to invoices, GRNs and purchase orders.
- Check subsequent payments to identify outstanding liabilities.
- Perform search for unrecorded liabilities.
- Test purchase and payable cut-off around year-end.

# 7. Fixed Assets

- Obtain the fixed asset register and reconcile it to the GL.
- Agree opening balances with prior-year audited balances.
- Test additions to invoices, payment records and approvals.
- Determine whether expenditure is correctly capitalized.
- Physically verify significant additions/assets where appropriate.
- Test disposals to disposal documents and sale proceeds.
- Recalculate depreciation for selected assets.
- Check useful lives and depreciation method.
- Review assets for impairment, damage or obsolescence.

# 8. Expenses

- Obtain detailed expense listing and reconcile to GL/TB.
- Perform analytical review against prior year and budget where available.
- Identify significant and unusual expense movements.
- Select samples based on value and risk.
- Check invoices and supporting documentation.
- Check management approval and payment evidence.
- Verify correct accounting classification.
- Check whether any capital expenditure has been incorrectly expensed.
- Perform expense cut-off testing.

# 9. Payroll

- Obtain annual/monthly payroll summary and reconcile to GL.
- Select employees for detailed testing.
- Check employment contracts and salary details.
- Recalculate gross salary, allowances and deductions.
- Agree selected salary payments to bank statements.
- Test new employees and supporting employment documents.
- Check terminated employees and final payments.
- Review unusual changes in payroll or employee numbers.

# 10. Loans & Borrowings

- Obtain loan/borrowing schedule and agree it to GL.
- Agree opening balances to prior-year financial statements.
- Obtain bank/financier confirmation.
- Check new loans against agreements and bank receipts.
- Check repayments against bank statements.
- Recalculate interest expense and accrued interest.
- Check current and non-current classification.
- Review loan terms, security and covenant requirements where applicable.
- Check subsequent repayments.

# 11. Equity / Share Capital

- Obtain share capital and equity movement schedule.
- Agree opening balances with prior-year audited FS.
- Agree share capital to company/CR records.
- Check additions, transfers or changes in shareholding.
- Check dividend declarations and payments.
- Reconcile retained earnings movement with profit/loss.
- Agree closing equity balances to financial statements.

# 12. Related Parties

- Obtain management's related-party listing.
- Check directors, shareholders and key management records.
- Review related-party transactions during the year.
- Review significant related-party balances.
- Confirm significant balances where appropriate.
- Check whether transactions are properly recorded.
- Verify required related-party disclosures in the financial statements.

# 13. Tax & Statutory Liabilities

- Obtain tax returns and tax computations.
- Reconcile tax balances with the GL/TB.
- Check tax payments against bank statements.
- Review outstanding tax liabilities and penalties.
- Review correspondence with tax authorities.
- Check tax provisions and current-year tax expense.
- Check relevant tax disclosures in the financial statements.

# 14. Journal Entries & Fraud

- Obtain journal entry listing for the audit period.
- Identify unusual, manual and high-value journal entries.
- Focus on journals posted close to year-end.
- Select samples based on risk.
- Check supporting documents and authorization.
- Review unusual journals affecting revenue, expenses or reserves.
- Check reversed or unusual post-year-end journals.
- Document any fraud indicators or management override concerns.

# 15. Analytical Review

- Compare current-year results with prior year.
- Analyse monthly revenue and expense trends.
- Compare gross profit and net profit margins.
- Analyse significant movements in major accounts.
- Calculate relevant ratios and key performance indicators.
- Review receivable, payable and inventory days where applicable.
- Investigate significant or unexpected fluctuations.
- Obtain management explanations and supporting evidence.

# 16. Going Concern

- Obtain management's going-concern assessment.
- Review current financial position and working capital.
- Review cash flow forecasts.
- Check expected cash inflows and major payments.
- Review loan repayments and financing facilities.
- Review losses, negative cash flows and overdue liabilities.
- Assess significant assumptions used in forecasts.
- Consider subsequent trading performance.
- Document the auditor's conclusion.

# 17. Subsequent Events

- Review post-year-end bank statements and transactions.
- Review significant sales, purchases and payments after year-end.
- Review board/management meeting minutes.
- Check new loans, investments or major asset purchases.
- Review litigation and significant legal developments.
- Discuss significant events with management.
- Determine whether events require adjustment or disclosure.
- Ensure relevant events are reflected in the financial statements.

# 18. Financial Statements & Disclosures

- Agree final financial statements to the audited Trial Balance.
- Check statement of financial position and profit/loss.
- Check cash flow statement and statement of changes in equity.
- Review accounting policies and significant estimates.
- Check comparative figures with prior-year audited FS.
- Check note disclosures and supporting schedules.
- Check related-party, tax and going-concern disclosures.
- Check subsequent-event disclosures.
- Perform final financial statement consistency and mathematical review.

# 19. Audit Differences & Adjustments

- Record all identified audit differences.
- Obtain management's response to proposed adjustments.
- Recalculate the financial statement impact of each difference.
- Update the adjusted and unadjusted misstatement schedule.
- Compare total unadjusted differences with materiality.
- Assess whether remaining differences affect the audit conclusion.
- Ensure all agreed adjustments are posted and reflected in the final TB.

# 20. Final Completion & Audit Report

- Ensure all audit sections are completed and cross-referenced.
- Ensure all review points have been cleared.
- Confirm all significant risks have been addressed.
- Review audit differences and final materiality assessment.
- Complete going-concern and subsequent-event procedures.
- Complete financial statement disclosure checklist.
- Obtain signed management representation letter.
- Perform final analytical review.
- Complete senior/manager/partner review.
- Finalize the auditor's report and signed financial statements.
